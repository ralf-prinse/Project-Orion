# TRADING STRATEGY

---

# Project Orion

## Mission

Orion is a deterministic decision-support system for short-term trading.

Its objective is **not** to predict the future.

Its objective is to continuously identify situations where the estimated probability of a favorable outcome justifies taking a trade within predefined risk limits.

Every recommendation must be explainable.

Artificial Intelligence never creates trading signals.

Artificial Intelligence explains deterministic signals generated by Orion.

---

# Primary Objective

For every monitored symbol Orion answers four questions:

1. Is this instrument worth watching?
2. Is this a good moment to enter?
3. Should the current position be maintained?
4. Is it time to exit?

---

# Trading Philosophy

The strategy follows several principles:

- Capital preservation comes first.
- Missing a trade is acceptable.
- Large losses are unacceptable.
- Small, repeatable gains are preferred over infrequent large gains.
- Every trade must have a predefined exit strategy.
- No trade may exist without a known maximum loss.

---

# Market Scope

Phase 1:

- Large-cap US equities
- Highly liquid ETFs
- Optional European large caps

Excluded initially:

- Penny stocks
- Illiquid securities
- Leveraged ETFs
- Options
- Futures
- Crypto

---

# Scanner Objective

The scanner continuously searches for:

- strong momentum;
- healthy liquidity;
- acceptable spread;
- controlled volatility;
- favorable risk/reward;
- no critical news events.

The scanner rejects instruments failing these conditions.

---

# Entry Requirements

A BUY candidate should satisfy multiple independent conditions.

Categories include:

## Liquidity

Minimum average daily volume.

## Spread

Bid/ask spread below configured threshold.

## Trend

Price aligned with prevailing trend.

## Momentum

Positive short-term momentum.

## Volatility

Enough movement to justify a trade, but not excessive instability.

## Relative Strength

Outperforming benchmark over the selected horizon.

## News

No significant negative news or corporate events likely to distort price behaviour.

## Market Context

Overall market conditions should not contradict the proposed direction.

---

# Position Sizing

Position size is determined by the deterministic PositionSizer.

Inputs include:

- available capital;
- maximum risk per trade;
- stop-loss distance;
- current exposure;
- confidence;
- volatility.

The UI and AI never calculate position size.

---

# Risk Management

Every trade requires:

- entry price;
- stop-loss;
- target price;
- estimated reward;
- estimated loss;
- risk/reward ratio.

Trades failing minimum requirements are rejected.

---

# Confidence

Confidence is derived from multiple deterministic components.

Examples:

- trend alignment;
- momentum quality;
- signal agreement;
- volatility quality;
- market regime;
- liquidity;
- historical reliability.

Confidence is never generated by AI.

---

# Exit Strategy

Every open position is continuously monitored.

Possible exit reasons include:

- target reached;
- stop-loss reached;
- trailing stop triggered;
- momentum deterioration;
- increased risk;
- adverse news;
- excessive holding time.

The first applicable deterministic exit rule generates the exit signal.

---

# AI Explainability

AI receives only deterministic outputs.

It may explain:

- why the trade qualifies;
- which factors contributed most;
- why confidence increased or decreased;
- why the signal changed.

AI may never override the deterministic engine.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Future versions may incorporate:

- additional indicators;
- improved ranking models;
- broker integrations;
- paper trading feedback;
- performance analytics.

New functionality must strengthen existing deterministic logic rather than replace it.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful Orion strategy:

- produces consistent, explainable signals;
- respects predefined risk limits;
- avoids unnecessary trades;
- adapts to changing market conditions through deterministic rules;
- remains transparent and auditable.
